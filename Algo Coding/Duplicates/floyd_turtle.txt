Idea:

1. When 'fast' and 'slow' somewhere in the cycle:

- 'slow' traveled E + d
- 'fast' traveled 2(E + d)
- E = # steps from START to CYCLE-ENTRY
- d = # steps from CYCLE-ENTRY to FAST-SLOW-MEET

2. The extra distance = nC, C = CYCLE-SIZE

2(E+d) - (E+d) = nC
<-> E + d = nC
-> E = nC - d

3. If the cycle length is C, and the initial gap between them 
(once both are in the cycle) is some value g where 0 < g < C,
then after exactly g iterations the gap hits 0 -- they're at the same node.

4. To find CYCLE-ENTRY: reset one ptr to START, keep other at MEET.
   Both move 1 step at a time.

- Ptr from START walks E steps -> lands on ENTRY.
- Ptr from MEET walks E = nC - d steps.
  It's d past ENTRY, so ends at d + (nC - d) = nC past ENTRY
  = full laps -> back at ENTRY.

Both collide at ENTRY = the duplicate.
